Architecture deepening report

bugjar 1.0.0 · 2026-11-12 · scope: bugjar/*.py after the review freeze. Method: deletion test, git-history-first reading, ranked candidates, then a grilling loop. This is a design artifact; the executable proof is the golden diff.

1. Where change actually lands

The frozen v1 tree was 6 modules and 1,375 lines. engine.py held 782 of them. Every new behavior in the review log — profile resolution, signal caveats, blob caps, the empty-world guard — landed in that one file. It owned five unrelated concerns: sandbox mechanics, channel semantics, delta debugging, report formatting, and orchestration. Its interface was as large as the implementation it hid. That is the definition of a shallow module.

3. Ranked candidates

StrongCandidate 1 — Runner seam plus four deep modules

Files: engine.py → runner.py, observation.py, minimizer.py, report.py.

Problem: one module owns five unrelated change drivers. A security fix in process limits sits next to Markdown prose generation. Reviewers cannot reason about locality.

Solution: put every sandbox concern behind runner.execute(), every channel/verdict semantic behind observation, reduction behind minimizer.ddmin, and read-only reporting behind report. engine becomes orchestration only.

Benefits: a test can now target the seam without spawning a process; the attack surface is one module instead of five; each module has one reason to change.

Deletion test: deleting runner.py forces temp dirs, rlimits, process groups, snapshots and blob collection back into three call sites. Complexity concentrates. Deep.

Worth exploringCandidate 2 — typed Cassette object instead of raw dicts

Problem: manifest is a dict passed through six functions; a misspelled key fails at runtime, not at load.

Solution: a Cassette dataclass with validated fields and to_manifest().

Benefit: schema errors become load-time errors.

Why deferred: it changes the module interface, and the brief forbids changing any function of the program. It needs its own release, not a structure-only refactor.

SpeculativeCandidate 3 — pluggable Runner implementations

Problem: replay currently assumes a local POSIX process.

Solution: an abstract Runner with container and remote backends.

Benefit: cross-platform replay.

Why rejected here: deleting the abstraction would cost nothing today. It is a shallow adapter until a second implementation exists. It also contradicts the documented boundary that the network and clock are outside the model.

5. Grilling loop (one question at a time, recommended answer attached)

Q: Are you changing behavior and calling it a refactor?

No. Recommended answer: freeze 34 CLI cases totaling 1,182 lines of scrubbed output, refactor, then require a byte-identical diff. Any behavior change is an automatic rejection. The diff is empty.

Q: Is runner.py actually deep, or just a renamed bucket?

Deep. Recommended answer: its interface is one call returning one ExecResult; it hides temp-dir lifecycle, setrlimit, process-group kills, symlink handling, snapshots and content-addressed blob collection. Deleting it forces all of that into three call sites.

Q: Why keep report.py separate if it only produces text?

Recommended answer: diff, explain and doctor share flattening, volatile-field filtering and profile comparison. Keeping them together means a schema change has one place to land, and every prose claim can be tied to a field path.

Q: Did you keep compatibility aliases that are dead code?

Three, not thirteen: _ddmin, _profile, _snapshot. They exist because the review probes in REVIEW_LOG.md call them by name, and that log must remain executable evidence. They are documented as compatibility aliases, not presented as API.

Q: What is the strongest objection to this refactor?

That it is mostly code movement, not a redesign. Correct. The brief forbade changing any function of the program. A structural deepening that preserves a 1,182-line golden matrix byte-for-byte is the largest change that constraint permits.
